CITES annotations — how they work

A CITES listing names a species and an Appendix. Many timber listings also carry an annotation — a number such as #15 or #17 written after the name. The annotation decides which parts and products of that species are controlled. Anything the annotation does not cover can be traded without CITES documents, even though the species itself is listed.

  • No annotation = every part and product is covered: logs, planks, furniture, carvings, chips, even small souvenirs (e.g. kosso, Pterocarpus erinaceus; all Appendix I species).
  • Narrow annotations (#5, #6, #17) cover only primary products — logs, sawn wood, veneer, plywood and (for #17) transformed wood such as decking. A finished chair made of mahogany (#6) needs no CITES permit.
  • Broad annotations (#1, #2, #4, #10, #15) cover almost everything, with a few listed exceptions such as seeds, retail-packaged goods, small finished items under 10 kg (#15), or musical instruments.

The Appendix decides what documents are needed: Appendix I — export permit and import permit, commercial trade essentially prohibited · Appendix II — export permit (or re-export certificate) from the exporting country · Appendix III — export permit from the listing country, certificate of origin from other countries.

Is this consignment covered? — 5 checks

  1. Species: does the wood match a CITES-listed taxon? (use this lookup and the 10x key; confirm with a laboratory if needed)
  2. Population: is the origin inside the listed population? e.g. Khaya, Afzelia, African Pterocarpus = Africa only; Cedrela, Swietenia macrophylla = Neotropics only; Diospyros = Madagascar only; Appendix III = listing country only. Plantation wood from outside the range is not covered.
  3. Product form: what is it — log, plank, veneer, plywood, decking, chips, oil, furniture, instrument? Use the HS code on the declaration (see matrix below).
  4. Annotation: does the annotation of that listing include this product form? Check the matrix.
  5. Documents: if covered, the correct CITES permit/certificate for the Appendix must accompany the shipment and match species, quantity and origin. If not covered — no CITES document required (other laws such as EUTR/EUDR or national export bans may still apply).
