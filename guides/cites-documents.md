# CITES documents required — Appendix I, II and III

Purpose: tell an officer **which CITES document must accompany a timber consignment**, what the issuing authority has certified, and what to check on the paper (or e-permit) in front of you.

> ⚠️ **Screening guide, not legal advice.** Based on the text of the Convention (Articles III–VII) and Resolution Conf. 12.3 (Rev.) on permits and certificates. National law can be stricter (see section 7). Confirm the live listing and annotation on [Species+](https://speciesplus.net) and with the national CITES Management Authority.

## 1. First question: is a CITES document needed at all?

A document is only needed if **all four** are true. Use the [species lookup](../docs/search.html) for steps 1–3.

1. The wood is a **listed taxon** (Appendix I, II or III).
2. The **origin is inside the listed population** (e.g. *Khaya* and *Afzelia* = Africa only; *Cedrela* = Neotropics only; Appendix III = listing country only).
3. The **product form is covered by the annotation** (e.g. #5 = logs, sawn wood, veneer sheets only; no annotation = everything).
4. It is **international trade** (export, import, re-export, or introduction from the sea). Transit and transhipment under customs control are exempt (section 6).

## 2. At a glance

| Situation | Appendix I | Appendix II | Appendix III |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Export** | Export permit — and an import permit must already have been issued | Export permit | Export permit **if exported from the country that listed the species**; otherwise a **certificate of origin** |
| **Import** | Import permit **plus** export permit or re-export certificate | Export permit or re-export certificate (CITES itself requires no import permit) | Export permit (from the listing country) or certificate of origin (from any other country); re-export certificate if re-exported |
| **Re-export** | Re-export certificate | Re-export certificate | Re-export certificate (accepted as proof that the rules were met) |
| **Introduction from the sea** | Certificate from the Management Authority of the State of introduction | Same, may cover up to one year's total | — |
| **Commercial trade** | Essentially prohibited (import must **not** be primarily commercial) | Allowed with permit | Allowed with permit |
| **Who certifies what** | Scientific Authority (non-detriment) **and** Management Authority (legal origin) in the export country; Scientific Authority in the import country | Scientific + Management Authority in the export country | Management Authority only (legal acquisition) |

## 3. Appendix by Appendix

### Appendix I — highest protection (CITES Art. III)
Timber taxa in this repository: *Dalbergia nigra*, *Abies guatemalensis*, *Araucaria araucana*, *Fitzroya cupressoides*, *Pilgerodendron uviferum*, *Podocarpus parlatorei*. None has an annotation, so **every part and product** is covered, including finished goods.

**Export permit** — the exporting country's authorities must be satisfied that:
- the Scientific Authority found the export will **not be detrimental** to the survival of the species;
- the specimen was **legally obtained**;
- (living specimens) it is shipped to minimise risk of injury or cruel treatment;
- an **import permit has been granted** by the importing country.

**Import permit** — the importing country's authorities must be satisfied that:
- its Scientific Authority advised the import will not harm the species;
- the specimen will **not be used for primarily commercial purposes**.

**Also required at import:** the export permit or re-export certificate. **Re-export:** re-export certificate, issued only if the specimen was imported in line with the Convention.

*Practical reading:* a commercial shipment of Appendix I timber with an ordinary "trade" export permit and no import permit is a major red flag. Legitimate Appendix I wood is typically pre-Convention stock (certificate required), or scientific/museum material.

### Appendix II — trade controlled (CITES Art. IV)
Most listed timber is here (*Dalbergia* spp., *Pterocarpus*, *Swietenia*, *Cedrela*, *Khaya*, *Afzelia*, ipê, *Gonystylus*, agarwood, *Pericopsis*, etc.).

**Export permit** — issued only if:
- the Scientific Authority found the export will not be detrimental to the species' survival;
- the specimen was **legally obtained**;
- (living specimens) shipped to minimise risk of injury.

**Import:** present the export permit or re-export certificate. **CITES does not require an import permit**, but many countries add one (section 7).
**Re-export:** re-export certificate (Management Authority satisfied the specimen was imported legally).
**Monitoring:** exporting countries must track permits issued against actual exports and may set limits (quotas) — so check quantity and any quota reference.

### Appendix III — one country asks for help (CITES Art. V)
A single country listed the species to get other countries' support. The document depends on **where the wood comes from**.

| Origin of the shipment | Document |
|---|---|
| The **listing country** | **Export permit** (Management Authority satisfied the specimen was legally obtained) |
| **Any other country** | **Certificate of origin** |
| **Re-export** | **Re-export certificate** from the re-exporting State |

Appendix III timber taxa in this repository:
- **Russia:** *Pinus koraiensis*, *Quercus mongolica*, *Fraxinus mandshurica* (annotation #5: logs, sawn wood, veneer sheets).
- **Nepal:** *Podocarpus neriifolius*, *Tetracentron sinense*, *Magnolia liliifera* var. *obovata* (annotation #1: broad).

*Example:* sawn Mongolian oak from Russia needs a Russian export permit; the same oak processed in and exported from another country needs that country's certificate of origin or re-export certificate.

## 4. What each document is

| Document | Issued by | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **Export permit** | Management Authority of the exporting country | Exports of Appendix I, II (and III from the listing country) |
| **Import permit** | Management Authority of the importing country | Appendix I imports (and where national law requires it) |
| **Re-export certificate** | Management Authority of the re-exporting country | Shipping previously imported specimens onwards |
| **Certificate of origin** | Management Authority of the country of origin | Appendix III exports from non-listing countries |
| **Certificate for introduction from the sea** | Management Authority of the State of introduction | Specimens taken in the marine environment (not relevant to timber) |
| **Pre-Convention certificate** | Management Authority of the exporting / re-exporting country | Specimens acquired before the species was listed |
| **Artificially propagated certificate** | Management Authority of the exporting country | Can replace the usual permits for artificially propagated Appendix I plants (Art. VII) |

## 5. Rules every genuine document should meet (Art. VI, Res. Conf. 12.3)

- **Valid six months** from the date of issue (export permits and re-export certificates). Import permits may be valid up to one year.
- Shows the **title of the Convention**, the **name and stamp of the issuing Management Authority**, and a **unique control number**.
- Follows the **standard CITES form** with a **security stamp** (number recorded on the form) and the issuing official's signature, seal and stamp legible.
- The **applicant's signature** must be present (missing signature = invalid).
- **One document per consignment.** It is cancelled and **retained by the importing country's Management Authority** after use — it cannot be reused.
- **Copies** must be clearly marked as copies and are not valid in place of the original.
- Species, quantity, origin and exporter/importer details must **match the shipment**.

### Source and purpose codes you will see on permits (timber)

| Code | Meaning | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| **W** | Taken from the wild | Normal for natural-forest timber |
| **A** | Artificially propagated (Appendix II/III, or non-commercial Appendix I) | Plantation claim — verify the plantation exists and the species is correct |
| **D** | Appendix I plants artificially propagated for commercial purposes (treated as Appendix II) | Rare for timber; verify carefully |
| **O** | Pre-Convention specimen | Must be supported by a pre-Convention certificate and date evidence |
| **I** | Confiscated or seized | Legal disposal of seized goods |
| **U** | Source unknown (must be justified) | Unusual — query it |
| **T** | Purpose: commercial | Expected for ordinary timber trade; **not acceptable for an Appendix I import** |
| **S / E / P / L** | Purpose: scientific / educational / personal / law enforcement-forensic | Check quantity fits the stated purpose |

## 6. Exemptions (Art. VII) — do not assume, verify

- **Transit and transhipment** under customs control: no CITES documents needed.
- **Pre-Convention specimens:** need a pre-Convention certificate from the export or re-export Management Authority.
- **Personal and household effects:** often exempt, but there are exceptions (notably Appendix I items and certain Appendix II items acquired abroad). Many countries apply their own thresholds.
- **Artificially propagated plants:** Appendix I species artificially propagated for commercial purposes are treated as Appendix II.
- **Scientific exchange:** non-commercial loans and exchanges between registered scientific institutions, with the authority's label.
- **Annotation exemptions** (e.g. finished products ≤ 10 kg under #15, finished musical instruments, retail-packaged goods) — these remove the product from control, so no document is needed. Always read the full annotation on Species+.

## 7. Stricter national measures (example: European Union)

CITES lets countries go further than the Convention (Art. XIV). The EU implements CITES through **Council Regulation (EC) No 338/97**, using Annexes A–D instead of the Appendices:

| CITES Appendix | EU Annex (generally) | Import into the EU requires |
|---|---|---|
| Appendix I | Annex A | **Import permit** + export permit / re-export certificate |
| Appendix II | Annex B | **Import permit** (stricter than CITES) + export permit / re-export certificate |
| Appendix III | Annex C | **Import notification** + export permit / certificate of origin |

Some species are placed in a higher Annex than their Appendix. Other EU rules (timber legality and deforestation-related regulations) may also apply **in addition** to CITES. Check your own national legislation.

## 8. Document check — what to verify on the paper

1. **Original or clearly marked copy?** Reject unauthenticated copies where an original is required.
2. **Issuing authority real and registered?** Compare name, stamp and format with known Management Authority specimens; check the **security stamp** and **control number**.
3. **Dates:** issued within the last six months and not yet expired; import permit within validity.
4. **Species name and Appendix** match the goods and the declaration. Genus-only or wrong-species wording (e.g. a permit for a non-listed look-alike) is a red flag.
5. **Quantity and units** (m³, kg, pieces) match the shipment; nothing over the permitted amount; partial shipments endorsed.
6. **Country of origin / exporter / consignee** match the transport and customs documents. Watch for laundering through a third country (mismatch between origin and export country, or a re-export certificate without the original export permit reference).
7. **Source and purpose codes** are plausible (see section 5).
8. **Annotation and product form:** does the permit describe the product form that the annotation covers (logs, sawn wood, veneer, plywood, transformed wood)?
9. **Already used?** An export permit cancelled or used for another consignment cannot be used again. Ask the issuing authority or check the electronic permit system.
10. **Alterations or erasures** not authenticated by the issuing authority.
11. **Electronic permits:** verify through the exporting country's e-permit system or the Management Authority rather than relying on a printout.

**If something does not match:** do not release the goods; record the document and consignment details, photograph the permit, take wood samples for laboratory confirmation ([field procedure](field-procedure.md)) and contact the national CITES Management Authority (and, where appropriate, the issuing country's authority) for verification.
